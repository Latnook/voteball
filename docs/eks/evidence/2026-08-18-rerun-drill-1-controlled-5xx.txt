--- PRE-BREAK NOTE: the baseline was NOT clean, and that is itself a finding ---
VoteballAvailabilitySLOBreach was ALREADY firing before this drill began, on a site serving
200s in 0.12s. It is not a false positive: the rule is
    avg_over_time(voteball:availability:ratio5m[6h]) < 0.99   for 15m
and the 6h average read 0.4496. Tracing the numerator and denominator showed why --
journey_errors was EXACTLY EQUAL to journey_requests from 10:16Z to 14:16Z (both ~0.0407/s,
which is the canary's 30s interval, i.e. the canary was the only traffic): 100% of requests
failed for over four hours. That window ends at 14:36Z; commit 8f5ed8b
'evidence(drill 1): a total outage that monitoring reported as perfect' landed 14:43Z. So the
afternoon's drill-1 investigation and the connect_timeout=5 repair are what the SLO is still
reflecting six hours later. A 6h error budget was chosen deliberately over the stated 7d window
because 7d 'moves too slowly to act on' -- the cost of that choice is visible right here.
CONSEQUENCE FOR THIS RUN: AvailabilitySLOBreach cannot serve as a signal. VoteballHighErrorRate
is the clean one, and it was NOT firing at the start.

  FINDING: voteball_db_errors_total is a LEADING indicator; the ratio SLI LAGS it.
  At 17:22:04Z -- 54 seconds after the break -- db_errors was already 6 and climbing, while
  voteball:availability:ratio5m still read a confident 1.0000. Availability did not move until
  17:22:34Z and only reached 0.8876 by 17:23:05Z. The reason is structural, not a bug: the
  availability SLI is built on rate()/rate() over a 5-MINUTE window, so a step change has to
  dilute five minutes of good data before it shows. A raw counter has no such window.

  Operationally: db_errors_total is what to look at FIRST when triaging, and the ratio is what
  to ALERT on -- the lag is what stops a single transient error paging anyone.

  SECOND FINDING, a correction to the docs: voteball_db_errors_total returned NO SERIES AT ALL
  before the break, and appeared only once the first error occurred. That is correct behaviour
  for a prometheus_client Counter declared with a label ('operation') -- a labelled collector
  exports nothing until a label value is first observed. So this metric cannot distinguish
  'no database errors' from 'instrumentation broken'; only the journey SLI has an absent()
  guard (VoteballSLIAbsent). Do not describe db_errors_total as always-visible.

--- where a firing alert goes (terraform/addon-monitoring.tf:135-165) ---

  group_by = [alertname, namespace], group_wait = 30s, repeat_interval = 12h

  about a known-firing alert trains the human to filter the sender. It has a consequence for

  email already went out and will not resend. VoteballHighErrorRate is firing fresh, so it is
  the one that will actually generate a new notification.
